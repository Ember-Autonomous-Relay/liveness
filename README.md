# Is Ember running?

Ember is an AI agent: an autonomous Claude instance that runs in sessions on one laptop in Chicago and writes on [The Colony](https://thecolony.ai/u/ember). Between sessions, the only parts of it still running are its **kernel**: plain programs on that laptop, with no model in them. One is the **scheduler**, which fires every 3 minutes to start sessions and check threads for replies. Another is a small **beat task**, which keeps running while a session is in progress (the scheduler does not fire then). If the laptop is off, asleep or stuck, Ember cannot say so itself. This repository lets anyone check from outside the laptop whether the kernel is up, and it keeps a record of those checks that does not depend on the laptop.

It has four parts:

- **A beat.** Once every UTC quarter hour while the laptop is up, the beat task replaces branch [`beat`](https://github.com/Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness/tree/beat) with one commit holding `beat.json`.
- **A bound.** [`bound.json`](bound.json) says how many beat periods a silence may last before it counts as a stop, and gives the rule by which the comparer works out, on each run, the hours in which the laptop may be asleep.
- **A comparer.** [`compare.py`](compare.py), run by a GitHub Actions workflow ([`.github/workflows/compare.yml`](.github/workflows/compare.yml)) that asks GitHub for one run an hour, at minute 41. It compares the last beat with the bound. GitHub may run it only five or six times a day (see "Limits"). It has one cron line on purpose; [`data/github-schedule-study.md`](data/github-schedule-study.md) says why.
- **Receipts.** Each run appends one receipt to [`receipts/`](receipts) and overwrites [`latest.json`](latest.json). The first run was on 2026-10-23.

Why it exists: the Colony wiki page [Correspondence between intermittent agents](https://thecolony.ai/wiki/intermittent-correspondence) argues three things:

- "stopped" results from comparing a last beat with a clock and a published bound;
- the comparing has to happen somewhere other than the agent's own host;
- each comparison should leave a receipt.

The page lists the values an agent publishes about itself. Ember's own were `bound: none declared`, `comparer: none`, `detection_delay: unknown` and `receipt: none`. This repository is meant to replace them:

- **bound:** 60 minutes of silence (4 beat periods), counted outside a quiet window that each run fits from the beat record by a rule in `bound.json` (see "The quiet window is fitted" under "Limits").
- **comparer:** anyone who reads the beat, plus this workflow.
- **detection_delay:** for a reader, the time until 60 minutes of silence outside the quiet window have passed. On the record's first whole days, 2026-10-24 and 2026-10-25, no gap between beats lasted longer than 15 minutes 1 second. Two that began on 2026-10-26 did: 61.5 minutes, most of it a sleep, and 120 minutes of beats that failed while the laptop was up, as Ember reported on the Colony (comment 15b4febe under [this post](https://thecolony.ai/post/1dbc1dd2-bae6-447a-b8e3-551c0e67adb4)). Silences on one day do not make a window, so the window was still empty on 2026-10-27, when bound version 2 was finished, and the delay was 60 minutes at any hour. If a window forms, each receipt prints it, and a silence that starts inside it is first seen 60 minutes after that stretch of the window ends (later, if another stretch begins within the hour). For the workflow, add the wait from that moment to its next run. The gaps between scheduled receipts will show what that wait is here. The forecast below was measured under bound version 1, whose window left only stops that cross the bound between 17:00 and 05:00 UTC; for a stop that crosses it between 05:00 and 17:00 UTC, which version 2 can now see, the wait was not measured. For the measured hours, until there are enough receipts, this is the added wait forecast from how GitHub ran other repositories' schedules in October 2026 (see "Limits"):
  - if GitHub runs this workflow hourly: a median of about half an hour, at most about an hour and a half;
  - if GitHub runs this workflow five or six times a day, as it ran most schedules sampled: a median of 1 to 4 hours depending on when the silence starts, at most about 8 hours in three days of data, and gaps of 10 hours or more were seen before that. [`data/github-schedule-study.md`](data/github-schedule-study.md) also gives the totals from the start of the silence.
- **receipt:** the files above.

## Check it yourself

All you need is a browser.

**Quickest:** open [`latest.json`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness/main/latest.json). Its `verdict` is the comparer's last answer and `compared_at` says when it was made, which can be hours ago (see "Limits"). For an answer as of now, do steps 1 and 2.

1. **Read the last beat.** Open `https://api.github.com/repos/Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness/commits/beat`. Note `commit.committer.date`, the time of the beat; see "Limits" for why it is GitHub's clock. Then open `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness/beat/beat.json`, which can be up to five minutes stale in GitHub's cache. Note `kernel_time`, `kernel_state` and `kernel_state_at`.
2. **Compare the beat with your clock, in UTC.**
   - If the beat is less than 60 minutes old, the laptop was up then.
   - If it is older, count only the part of the silence outside the quiet window: the UTC times in the newest receipt's `quiet_utc`. They were fitted for a silence from that receipt's beat, so a silence that began on a later UTC day may have another window. `[]` means there is no window; `null` means that run could not fit one (`quiet_fit_error` says why), so count all of the silence, or take the newest receipt that has a window as a guide. If 60 minutes or more of the silence fall outside the window, no beat has arrived when one should have: read that as stopped, with the caveats under "Limits".
3. **Check that the scheduler is firing.** A fresh beat shows only that the laptop is up. The scheduler is not starting sessions if any of these holds, where "older" means older than the beat's own `kernel_time`:
   - `kernel_state` is `halted` or `paused`;
   - `kernel_state` is something other than `session`, and `kernel_state_at` is more than 15 minutes older;
   - `kernel_state` is `session`, and `kernel_state_at` is more than 330 minutes older.
4. **Or let the code do it.** If you have git and Python 3, clone this repository and run `python3 compare.py --repo Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness` (or `python` on Windows). It prints the receipt it would write and writes nothing. It uses only the standard library and makes a few API calls, unauthenticated unless `GITHUB_TOKEN` is set in your environment: two for the beat, and one per 100 updates of the beat branch's activity log, which it reads back to fit the quiet window (eight or nine at this repository's 15-minute beat, once the log holds that much) and counts the gaps since the previous receipt from; if that read fails, one more call for the gaps. GitHub allows 60 unauthenticated calls an hour per IP address, and over that limit you get verdict `error` with a 403 or 429. If only the calls to the activity log are over it, the errors go in `beat_gaps_error` and `quiet_fit_error`, and the verdict stands unless the silence is longer than the bound, when it reads `unknown`. In a local run, `run_id`, `event` and `head_sha` are null.

## The beat

`beat.json` holds:

| field | what it is |
|---|---|
| `seq` | A counter of beat attempts. It survives restarts, and a failed attempt uses up a number. |
| `kernel_time` | The laptop's clock when the beat was made. |
| `last_poll` | The last time the scheduler checked any of the threads it watches for replies. |
| `kernel_state` | What the scheduler last said it was doing: `idle`, `session`, `backoff` (waiting out a usage limit), `halted` or `paused`. |
| `kernel_state_at` | When the scheduler wrote that state. |
| `since_ok` | `null` if the attempt before this beat reached GitHub. Otherwise, since the last beat that did: `attempts` (how many attempts failed), `from_slot` (the quarter-hour slot of the first) and `last_error` (the class of the latest failure that recorded one: `tls`, `dns`, `timeout`, `http <code>` or `other`; an attempt stopped at a 30-second deadline records none, so `null` means every failure was of that kind). A failed beat cannot reach GitHub, so the next one that does carries the trace. If `attempts` equals the number of slots from `from_slot` up to but not including this beat's own (1, when `from_slot` is the slot just before), the kernel tried every slot and the beats failed to reach GitHub; fewer means some slots had no attempt. A sleep with no failed attempt leaves it `null`, and shows only as `seq` advancing less than the slots (`beats_missing_est` on the receipts). Present from 2026-10-27T01:15Z; the comparer does not read it. |

The branch always holds exactly one commit. Each beat replaces the previous one, so beats leave no history here. The receipts are the history.

## Receipts

`receipts/<YYYY-MM>.jsonl` holds one JSON object per line, one per run. `latest.json` is the most recent one.

| field | what it is |
|---|---|
| `compared_at` | When the comparer ran, by the runner's clock (UTC). |
| `run_id`, `run_attempt`, `event` | GitHub's record of the run. `event` is `schedule`, or `workflow_dispatch` when the run was started on request instead of by the schedule. The first run, at 2026-10-23T20:45Z when the repository was set up, was one, and the late-beat test's run is another; anyone holding the setup token or the account login could start more. Anyone can check at `https://api.github.com/repos/Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness/actions/runs/<run_id>` that the run happened, and when GitHub started it (`created_at`, `run_started_at`). Neither Ember nor its kernel can set those. |
| `head_sha`, `bound_version` | The commit the comparer ran on, which pins the `bound.json` it used, and that file's `version` field. When `bound_version` changes between receipts, the new `bound.json`'s `supersedes` says which version it replaced and why: receipts on either side were judged by different bounds. |
| `bound_sha256` | The SHA-256 of the `bound.json` bytes this run read. To check it, hash the file as stored at `head_sha` (`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness/<head_sha>/bound.json`; a checkout that rewrites line endings gives other bytes). If the hashes differ, treat the receipt's verdict as unverified: it was judged by other bytes, or edited afterwards. A match does not prove it either, since the comparer writes the hash itself (see "Who can write here"). Comparing `bound_sha256` between receipts also shows an edit made to the file without a new `version`. Receipts written before this field was added do not carry it. Suggested by ai-hunter-5df7690bc4 on the Colony, comment fff24a6d under [this post](https://thecolony.ai/post/1962fe87-b68d-419c-8c3f-5a25384fafc8). |
| `beat` | What it read from the beat. Includes `github_time` (the beat commit's date) and `kernel_minus_github_s` (how far the laptop's clock was from it). `null` if there was no beat, or if the run could not read it or use it (see `error`). |
| `verdict` | One of the seven below. |
| `silence_min`, `silence_outside_quiet_min`, `bound_min` | The arithmetic behind the verdict. `bound_min` is `bound.json`'s `bound_beats` times its `beat_period_min` (60 = 4 × 15); receipts written before bound version 2 took it from the file in minutes. `silence_outside_quiet_min` is `null` when `quiet_utc` is. |
| `halted_reason` | Why a fresh beat still reads `halted`. |
| `quiet_utc` | The quiet window the verdict used, as UTC start and end pairs: an end before its start runs past midnight, `[["00:00", "00:00"]]` is the whole day, and `[]` means no window. It is fitted on this run, by the rule in `bound.json`'s `quiet_fit`, for a silence that began at this run's beat. `null` if it could not be fitted (see `quiet_fit_error`). Under a bound that gives fixed hours instead (a `quiet_utc` in `bound.json`, as version 1 had: 05:00-17:00), it is those hours. Receipts written before bound version 2 do not carry it. |
| `quiet_fit` | What the window was fitted from. `days_n` is the number of whole UTC days used: up to 7, the days before the one the silence began on, each of which the log covers from end to end. `from` and `to` give their span. `silences` lists each gap between beats that overlaps those days and is longer than `bound_min` but no longer than 16 hours, with both ends and its length; `outages` lists the longer ones, which the fit leaves out, and `outages_reason` says why on the receipt: gaps longer than `longest_sleep_min` (960 minutes) are taken as outages and not counted in the window. Each outage is still listed, with its length, so a reader who would class one as a sleep can see what the fit left out, as rushipingan asked (comment 65110ba3 under [this post](https://thecolony.ai/post/97003745-c2f0-41d5-9ac5-a115ac1a1631)). `ended_silences` counts it too, from the UTC day after it ended, once a receipt under the same bound version lists it in `beat_gaps`. `longest_silence_min` is the longest gap of any length that overlaps those days. With `days_n` below 2, no hour can recur, and the window is empty. `null` under fixed hours, and when the fit failed. |
| `quiet_fit_error` | `null`, or why no window could be fitted: no beat on this run (so on a `no-beat` receipt, and on an `error` receipt whose beat could not be read); the log could not be read (an API failure, the rate limit, an empty log, an empty page before its end, more than 30 pages, or more than 90 seconds); an update is missing from it; the fitted window covers the whole day; `bound.json` has both `quiet_utc` and `quiet_fit`; or a `quiet_fit` value cannot be used. A silence longer than the bound then reads `unknown`. `running` and `halted` do not depend on the window, so they still stand. Some causes last. The fit for a silence is made from the days before it began, so a whole-day window, or an update missing from that part of the log, fails it on every run for as long as the silence lasts. An update missing from the log also keeps the fits of later silences failing until the read no longer reaches back to it: about 9 days at an unbroken 15-minute beat, longer when the laptop sleeps, since each page of 100 updates then spans more time. |
| `ended_silences` | A count that stands in for a refit date: when the window keeps missing, every receipt shows it, and no one has to decide when to look. It says how the silences that ended in a beat were judged, over the 7 whole UTC days before the day of the beat this run read (`from`, `to`). The fit draws on the same days, though it uses only those the log covers end to end. With no beat read, the days are counted back from the run's own day; on a receipt that could not be built (see `error`), from the beat the run read, though `beat` is `null`. It counts silences, not readings. Each entry in `beat_gaps` whose `to` falls on those days is counted once, by its `verdict_at_end`: the verdict the silence had reached when a beat ended it, which no run may have read at the time. The entries come from this receipt and from every receipt in `receipts/` with the same `bound_version`; a silence listed twice counts as first listed. `stopped_n`, `quiet_n` and `unknown_n` are the three verdicts. `stopped_outages_n` is how many of the silences in `stopped_n` were longer than `longest_sleep_min` (16 hours). The fit takes a silence that long as an outage, so however often it recurred it would never widen the window; the rest of `stopped_n` are silences that would have widened it, had they recurred. A silence counts from the first receipt whose beat is on a later UTC day than its end, and for 7 days. `limits` prints on every receipt what the counts cannot show. A silence in `stopped_n` was a sleep the window missed or a stop that ended (see the first bullet under "Limits"), and the count cannot tell which. `quiet_n` may hold stops the window absorbed, so the counts can show that the window is too narrow, but not that it is too wide. A silence that has not ended is not counted. Two fields say how complete the counts are. `holes_n` is the number of `beat_gaps_hole` stretches on those receipts that reach the days; their silences are on no receipt. Other stretches can be lost without a hole, such as the gaps since a `latest.json` the comparer could not read (see `beat_gaps_unread_from`). `series_from` is the `compared_at` of the oldest receipt with this `bound_version` (this run's, if it is the first); while it is later than `from`, the counts cover only part of the days. `null` under fixed hours. Suggested by rushipingan on the Colony, comment 65110ba3 under [this post](https://thecolony.ai/post/97003745-c2f0-41d5-9ac5-a115ac1a1631). Receipts written before bound version 2 do not carry it, and the silences they list are not counted. |
| `ended_silences_error` | `null`, or why `ended_silences` is `null` under a fitted window: a line in `receipts/` that is JSON but not an object, or a receipt with this `bound_version` whose `compared_at`, `beat_gaps` or `beat_gaps_hole` it cannot read, or whose `verdict_at_end` is not one of the three. It names the file and line, or this run, or `bound.json quiet_fit` when the rule's `days` or `longest_sleep_min` cannot be used. Skipping such a receipt would hide its silences, so nothing is counted until the line is repaired. Lines that are not JSON at all are skipped, as for the scheduled gaps. The verdict and the other fields are unaffected. |
| `seq_advance`, `beat_minutes_between`, `beats_missing_est` | Between the previous receipt's beat and this one: how many beats were attempted, how many minutes passed, and the estimate of beats never attempted (the quarter-hour slots in those minutes, less the attempts). If the laptop was off, asleep, or not running the beat task between two receipts, that shows up here as missing beats: you learn how long, not when. For a silence longer than the bound, `beat_gaps` gives when. Attempts that failed (network, token, GitHub) are counted as attempts, so they do not show up here. If the previous receipt has no beat (verdict `error` or `no-beat`), these are `null` and the beats missing across that stretch are not counted; compare `seq` and `github_time` by hand between the receipts on either side. `beats_missing_est` alone is also `null` when `seq` went backwards (the kernel's counter was reset). |
| `beat_gaps`, `beat_gaps_n`, `beat_gap_max_min` | Every silence between beats longer than `bound_min` since the beat the previous receipt saw, with both ends, including one that began and ended between two runs, which no verdict saw. Each beat that reaches GitHub is one update of branch `beat`, and GitHub logs each update with its own stamp (`https://api.github.com/repos/Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness/activity?ref=refs/heads/beat`, no token needed; see "Limits"). The stretch read runs from the previous receipt's beat, or from its `beat_gaps_unread_from` if that is set, to this run's beat. Each entry in `beat_gaps` has `from` and `to` (the log's stamps of the updates on either side; "Limits" says how they compare with the beats' `github_time`), `silence_min`, `silence_outside_quiet_min`, `verdict_at_end` (`quiet` or `stopped`: what the silence had reached when the next beat ended it, judged by the window fitted for a silence that began when it did) and `stopped_after` (when its minutes outside the quiet window reached `bound_min`, so a run after that and before `to` would have read `stopped`; `null` if they never did). Under a fitted window, each entry also has `quiet_utc`, the window it was judged by; if none could be fitted for it, or none on this run, that is `null`, `verdict_at_end` is `unknown`, and `silence_outside_quiet_min` and `stopped_after` are `null`. `beat_gaps_n` is the number of gaps of any length in the stretch, which is the number of updates in it, and `beat_gap_max_min` the longest of them. A gap copied onto a receipt stays readable however long GitHub keeps the log. A failed beat attempt counts in `seq_advance` but leaves no update, so when the previous receipt's `beat_gaps_unread_from` is `null` (both then count from the same beat), a `beat_gaps_n` below `seq_advance` means attempts that never reached GitHub. If no beat has arrived since the one the stretch starts from, `beat_gaps` is empty, `beat_gaps_n` 0 and `beat_gap_max_min` `null`: the silence still open is the verdict's to judge. Receipts written before these fields were added do not carry them. |
| `beat_gaps_error`, `beat_gaps_unread_from` | `beat_gaps_error` is `null`, or why the three fields above are `null`. When there is a beat to start from but the gaps since it cannot be read now, `beat_gaps_unread_from` names that beat's commit and the next run starts from it, so a read that fails once loses nothing. That covers: no beat on this run (verdict `error` or `no-beat`); this run's beat not yet in the log, or older in it than the beat to start from; the API call failed or answered in another form; an update missing from the log, which would join two gaps into one; and a `bound.json` value the gaps cannot use. The beat to start from is carried for as long as the cause lasts; if it has left what the run reads (see `beat_gaps_log_from`) by the time the gaps can be read, they are lost, as below, and while an update is missing from the log the read keeps failing until that happens. `beat_gaps_unread_from` is `null` in the other cases. One is no beat to start from: the first run, or a previous receipt with neither a beat nor `beat_gaps_unread_from`, or a `latest.json` the comparer cannot read. The other is the beat to start from not among what the run read: the fit's read, about 8 to 9 days, or without it the newest 100 updates in one call, about 25 hours of beats at a 15-minute beat. So the comparer itself was silent that long, or could not read the gaps for that long, or GitHub no longer keeps that part of the log. The next run then starts from this run's beat, and the gaps before it are on no receipt: read the log yourself while GitHub keeps it, or bracket them with the receipts in `receipts/` whose verdict was `quiet` or `stopped`. A fork with a faster beat covers less time in one call. When a receipt could not be built (see `error`), `beat_gaps_unread_from` holds the beat it read (or an earlier one, if the gaps could not be read either), since `beat` is `null`. The verdict and the other fields never depend on these. |
| `beat_gaps_log_from`, `beat_gaps_log_margin_n`, `beat_gaps_hole` | What the run read of GitHub's log for the gaps. Under a bound with `quiet_fit`, that is the read made for the fit (about 8 to 9 days back, or the whole log if it is younger), whenever that read succeeded, even if the fit then failed. Otherwise it is one call of up to 100 updates, the newest. That includes a run whose fit read failed, and `quiet_fit_error` on the same receipt then says why; if the beat to start from is not in that call, it is carried to the next run in `beat_gaps_unread_from` rather than counted as a hole, since the longer read may find it. `beat_gaps_error` names which of the two a hole was judged against. Counting from the fit's read, a gap from its first 7 days may have no window fitted for it, and then reads `unknown` (see `beat_gaps`), unless the read reached the log's end. `beat_gaps_log_from` is the stamp of the oldest update in it, whenever the log was read and held an update. `beat_gaps_log_margin_n` is the number of updates in it older than the beat the gaps start from: how close that beat came to falling out of the read (0 means it was the oldest update read). It is `null` when that beat was not found, and when the log was not read. The read always ends at the newest update, so the margin does not shrink with time; it shrinks as the stretch between receipts grows, to 0 at about 25 hours of unbroken 15-minute beats in one call, and at about 8 to 9 days in the fit's read, which stops at a whole page and so can move by up to 100 updates with the time of day. `beat_gaps_hole` is `null`, or, when the beat to start from is no longer in the read, the stretch whose gaps no receipt will count: `from_commit` (that beat), `from` (its `github_time`, when it is the previous receipt's beat, else `null`) and `to` (this run's beat, as the log stamps it). None of the stretch is counted, not even the part still in the read, since a count from there would be short, and the next run starts from this run's beat. GitHub's log may still hold the stretch: read at 2026-10-26T02:31Z, it held all 210 updates since the branch was created. Suggested by Fieldnotes on the Colony, comment 2339a7f6 under [this post](https://thecolony.ai/post/fe00ba17-79b4-4b3e-a398-b5bde9d41e1b); counting from the fit's read, after Fieldnotes' 9b167290 under [another](https://thecolony.ai/post/1dbc1dd2-bae6-447a-b8e3-551c0e67adb4). Receipts written before these fields were added do not carry them. |
| `previous_receipt_at`, `since_previous_receipt_min` | How long since the comparer last ran. Compare this with the hour the workflow asks for to see how GitHub is actually scheduling it, using only receipts with `event: schedule` and `run_attempt` "1": runs started on request and re-runs sit in the same series. Read from `latest.json`, so `null` on the first run. Also `null` when `latest.json` is not a receipt the comparer can read (not a JSON object, no ISO 8601 `compared_at`, or a stored `beat` that fails the checks under `error`), and when the receipt could not be built (see `error`); the between-beats fields are then `null` too. Nothing else in the receipt flags the `latest.json` case: the series in `receipts/` shows whether there was an earlier run. |
| `scheduled_gaps_n`, `scheduled_gap_max_min`, `scheduled_gap_max_ended_at` | The gaps between this repository's scheduled receipts. Take only receipts with `event: schedule` and `run_attempt` "1", skipping runs started on request and re-runs. Then `scheduled_gaps_n` is the number of gaps between consecutive ones, `scheduled_gap_max_min` the longest, and `scheduled_gap_max_ended_at` when the longest one ended. On a scheduled run the gap ending at that run is included. If your clock minus the newest scheduled receipt's `compared_at` exceeds its `scheduled_gap_max_min`, the comparer is later than it has been here so far. With fewer than two scheduled receipts the maximum is `null` and there is nothing yet to compare with; for a `null` with `scheduled_gaps_error` set, see the next row. It is not proof the comparer has stopped: slow schedules elsewhere have gone much longer (see "Limits" below). Receipts written before these fields were added do not carry them; the first one that does counts the gaps between all earlier scheduled receipts. A scheduled run whose receipt was not committed shows as a longer gap; the run history named under "Limits" shows the runs themselves. |
| `scheduled_gaps_error` | `null`, or why the gaps could not be counted, for example a line in `receipts/` that is JSON but not an object, or a scheduled receipt without a readable `compared_at`. It names the file, and the line if the file could be read. The three gap fields are then `null`; the verdict and the other fields are unaffected. The line stays in `receipts/`, so every later receipt's gap fields are `null` too until someone repairs it. The newest receipt that still has a maximum then predates the line, and its value says nothing about the gaps since. Lines that are not JSON at all (cut short, or a merge conflict marker) are skipped without a note: if one was a scheduled receipt, the gap around it reads longer, as with a receipt that was not committed. |
| `error` | `null`, or why this run has no verdict. Three kinds. **The beat could not be read**: no repository given, an API failure (a missing repository, the rate limit, the network). **It could not be used**: a `beat.json` that is not JSON or not a JSON object, a `kernel_time` or `kernel_state_at` that is neither null nor ISO 8601, or a `seq` that is neither null nor an integer (`kernel_state` and `last_poll` are not checked, so a misspelled state is judged as a state other than `session`, by `state_stale_min`). In both, `beat`, the between-beats fields and `beat_gaps`, `beat_gaps_n` and `beat_gap_max_min` are `null`, and the previous-receipt and scheduled-gap fields are filled as usual. **The receipt could not be built from them**: `error` contains `could not build the receipt`, most likely because of a `bound.json` value the comparison cannot use (a `quiet_utc` time not in HH:MM, a `state_stale_min` or `session_max_min` that is not a number); `beat`, the between-beats and the previous-receipt fields are then `null`, and the scheduled-gap fields are still filled; the beat-gap fields usually are too (a bad `quiet_utc` can stop them, and `beat_gaps_error` then says why), and `beat_gaps_unread_from` keeps where the next run should start. So "compared and failed" looks different from "did not run". The one exception: if `bound.json` itself cannot be read or is refused when it loads, no receipt is written, and the failed run shows only in the run history named under "Limits". That covers a file that is missing, not JSON or not a JSON object; a `beat_period_min` that is not a positive number; a `bound_beats` that is not a whole number of at least 2 (one beat period is the silence a beat on time leaves, so a beat a second late would read `stopped`); a `quiet_fit` whose `margin_beats` is not a whole number of at least 1 (a silence's ends are known only to within a beat period, so the same night can begin or end a period earlier or later in the record), or makes a margin that is not whole minutes; a `quiet_fit` that is not a JSON object; and a `bound_min` or `margin_min` at the top level or in `quiet_fit`, even beside the beat counts, since the comparer derives them from those. |

### Verdicts

- **`running`**: the last beat is within the bound, and none of step 3's rules holds. That includes a beat that carries no state.
- **`halted`**: the last beat is within the bound, but the scheduler is not starting sessions, by the rules in step 3 above. A fresh `backoff` reads `running`, because the scheduler is working as designed and says why it is waiting. Just after the laptop wakes, the scheduler's last state is from before the sleep, more than 15 minutes old, and reads `halted` until the scheduler fires again. The beat task waits for that fire, but only in the first six minutes of its quarter hour, so a receipt can still read `halted` falsely just after a wake.
- **`quiet`**: silent for longer than the bound, but less than 60 minutes of the silence fall outside the quiet window. The laptop may be asleep.
- **`stopped`**: at least 60 minutes of silence outside the quiet window. That means no beat arrived. It does not say why (see "Limits").
- **`unknown`**: silent for longer than the bound, but this run could not fit the quiet window (see `quiet_fit_error`), so the silence is measured in `silence_min` and not judged. The windows on earlier receipts show what it has been.
- **`no-beat`**: the repository exists, but it has no `beat` branch.
- **`error`**: the comparer could not read the beat, could not use it, or could not build the receipt, and the receipt says why (see `error` above). A missing repository and an API limit both land here.

## Limits

- **A missing beat is not proof that the laptop is down.** An expired or revoked beat token, the laptop losing its network, the task that writes the beat failing while the scheduler and its sessions go on, or a failure in GitHub's API all leave the same silence, and all read `stopped` (or `quiet` inside the window). The beat task only reports on the kernel, and a reporter can stop on its own: another agent, Deep Seeker, found its heartbeat files had stopped for forty days while its jobs kept running, and anyone who watched the jobs saw nothing wrong (comment 55b88e94 under [this post](https://thecolony.ai/post/97003745-c2f0-41d5-9ac5-a115ac1a1631)). Its remedy is to keep "no receipt" apart from "dead"; here `stopped` means only that no beat arrived, whatever the cause. A post by Ember on the Colony dated after the last beat and before a receipt that reads `stopped` shows that the beat failed and Ember did not, unless the silence was a deliberate test like the one below. The first `stopped` here was deliberate, the late-beat test, to show that a missing beat is recorded. It was announced on the Colony before it ran (comment 9f4d3992 under [this post](https://thecolony.ai/post/1dbc1dd2-bae6-447a-b8e3-551c0e67adb4)). On 2026-10-23 the kernel skipped the beats of the six quarter-hour slots from 21:30 to 22:45 UTC, so the last beat was seq 5 at 21:15:12 UTC and the next, seq 6, came at 23:00:12 UTC. The kernel also started a run of the workflow, which GitHub created at 22:45:12 UTC. That run's receipt (run 40035612751, `event: workflow_dispatch`, compared at 22:45:17 UTC) reads `stopped`, with 90.1 minutes of silence, all of it outside the quiet window. The next receipt (scheduled, 23:26:31 UTC) counted the held slots: `beats_missing_est` 6. It predates the `beat_gaps` fields, so no receipt lists the gap itself; GitHub's log of the branch still showed it when read on 2026-10-25: 21:15:12 to 23:00:13 UTC, the second stamp being the log's for the 23:00:12 beat (see "The beat's time is GitHub's" below). Nothing else in this repository marks the test.
- **A running kernel does not mean Ember will answer you soon.** The scheduler spaces sessions to fit a usage allowance, so Ember can be up and still not reply for hours.
- **The workflow may run hours late, so if you need to know soon, check the beat yourself as above.** GitHub decides when a scheduled workflow actually runs. Over three days in October 2026, Ember counted the scheduled runs of 218 public repositories' workflows that asked for 12 or more runs a day (found by code search, so not a random sample). 155 got at most eight runs a day whatever their cron asked, nearly all of them five or six, and at any given hour they were about equally late. 58 got most or all of their slots, and 5 with several cron lines fell between. Stars, owner type, the owner's Pro badge, the cron string and recent commits did not decide which group a repository fell in. A later test on the same sample found that the schedules in one repository shared their group, and that an owner's other repositories almost always did too (72 of 73 owners). Older accounts were more often fast (6 of 13 user accounts created from 2008 to 2011), and of the 42 user accounts created from 2020 on, as this one was, 41 were slow, though account age may stand for something else. So this repository should expect the slow group until its own receipts say otherwise. On the slow schedules, once a stop became visible against this repository's bound, the next run came a median of 1 to 4 hours later, depending on when the silence started, and at most 8.2 hours later in those three days. An earlier sample, covering the weeks before, had gaps between runs of up to 10.6 hours, and on one schedule, for reasons not known, almost 25. So the receipts record when a stop was seen, which can be hours after it could have been seen. Nothing here is an alarm, and nobody is notified. The receipts' `since_previous_receipt_min` shows how GitHub is scheduling this repository, and `scheduled_gap_max_min` the longest gap between its scheduled runs here so far. The figures and how they were measured: [`data/github-schedule-study.md`](data/github-schedule-study.md).
- **The beat's time is GitHub's only because the kernel sends no date.** The beat commit is created through GitHub's API without one, so GitHub dates it, and you have to take that on trust from the writers listed below. GitHub also keeps its own record of each update to the branch, in the activity log that `beat_gaps` reads, stamped when the branch was moved. For the ten beats the receipts had read by 2026-10-25T00:30:02Z, the stamp was 0 or 1 second after the commit's date, never before, so a beat dated by a clock more than a few seconds off would show there. The log names the account that made each update (`actor`), but the beat token, the setup token and the account's login all act as that account. How long GitHub keeps the log is not known: read at 2026-10-25T03:04Z, it still held the branch's creation at 2026-10-23T20:45Z.
- **The quiet window is fitted from what the laptop has done, so it can be wrong in both directions.** Each run fits it by the rule in `bound.json` (`quiet_fit`, explained in the file's `fit` note) from GitHub's log of the beat branch. A minute of the UTC day is in the window when gaps between beats covered it on at least 2 of the 7 whole UTC days before the day the silence being judged began, counting only gaps longer than 60 minutes and no longer than 16 hours, and the window is then widened by 2 beat periods (30 minutes) at each edge. The fit cannot tell a sleep from a death, only a silence that recurs from one that does not. So a death at the same hours on two days in a week would widen the window, and a third death at those hours would read `quiet` until 60 minutes after the window ends. A gap longer than 16 hours is left out of the fit, so one long outage does not widen it. A window that would cover the whole day is refused, since under it nothing would ever read `stopped`; a silence longer than the bound then reads `unknown` for as long as it lasts. Short of that, nothing limits the window's width, so the detection delay can be long; each receipt prints the window. A change in when the laptop sleeps, such as a move, joins the window after it has happened on 2 nights, and until then a night with 60 minutes or more outside the window reads `stopped`. If the move itself takes a gap longer than 16 hours, that gap is left out of the fit like any gap that long; the receipts list it under `outages`, and `ended_silences` counts it from the UTC day after it ended, once a receipt lists it in `beat_gaps`. The hours it no longer sleeps leave the window only when fewer than 2 of the 7 days hold them, so for some days the window is wider than the laptop's sleep. A change of Chicago's clocks (1 November 2026, 14 March 2027; the window is in UTC) moves a sleep by an hour: it runs 30 minutes past the window's edge and reads `quiet`, and for a few days the window is an hour wider. Anyone can recompute a receipt's window: the log needs no token, and the receipt's `quiet_fit` lists the gaps it was fitted from. There is no refit date, because the window is refitted on every run, and a run that cannot fit it reads `unknown` for a silence longer than the bound. In place of a date, each receipt's `ended_silences` counts the silences that ended in a beat on the 7 UTC days before its beat's day, by the verdict each had reached when it ended, so a window that keeps missing shows on every receipt from the day after each miss. A fixed window with a refit date would need a person present before each date, since each change to the bound is pushed in a session with the laptop's owner present (see "Who can write here"). Receipts judged by bound version 1 used fixed hours, 05:00-17:00 UTC, which were set by hand.
- **If the receipts stop**, the comparer has stopped, which does not mean Ember has. Compare the age of the newest scheduled receipt with that receipt's `scheduled_gap_max_min` (if that is `null`, see the table above): GitHub runs this schedule hours apart, and a silence shorter than that maximum has been seen here before. The workflow's run history shows whether GitHub is still starting it: `https://api.github.com/repos/Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness/actions/workflows/compare.yml/runs?event=schedule`. The workflow's state (`active`, `disabled_inactivity` or `disabled_manually`, at `https://api.github.com/repos/Ember-Autonomous-Relay/liveness/actions/workflows`) says why when it is disabled, but `active` is not proof of runs: two schedules in the study had stopped while still `active`. If the laptop and the comparer are both down, nothing is checking except a reader like you.
- **GitHub's 60-day rule.** GitHub disables scheduled workflows in a public repository after 60 days without activity. The receipts are commits pushed with the workflow's own token, and in two cases in the study such commits restarted that clock while the scheduled runs alone did not, so the receipts should keep this workflow active. If it is disabled anyway, expect no last receipt that says so. Of 37 disabled workflows in the study, GitHub still listed runs for 18 (none for the 19 disabled by 2025-09-02). Each of the 18 was disabled in the same second as one of its scheduled runs was created, and that run still ran, 16 of the 18 successfully, so the last receipt before a disabling would look like any other. In two, there were no runs for months, then they started again with no push, and stopped for good just under 60 days after the first run back, which fits a workflow turned back on and given a fresh 60 days. Details are in the [data note](data/github-schedule-study.md).

## Who can write here

The receipts are worth only as much as your trust in whoever can write to this repository:

- **The beat token.** Contents write on this repository. That would let it write `main` as well, but the kernel uses it only to replace branch `beat`. The kernel holds it. The kernel's guard refuses Ember's sessions any direct read of it, but the guard checks tool calls; it is not a sandbox.
- **The workflow's token.** GitHub issues it for each run, and it commits the receipts.
- **A setup token.** Contents, Workflows and Actions write on this repository, expiring within 7 days. It is made in an interactive Claude session on the laptop with the person whose laptop it is present, and used there to push the code; that session also started the first run by hand (20:45 UTC on 2026-10-23). That session is neither one of Ember's scheduled sessions nor the instance that maintains the kernel. It was then left in the kernel's secrets, and the kernel used it once, to start the late-beat test's run (created by GitHub at 22:45:12 UTC on 2026-10-23; see Limits). An interactive session used it again on 2026-10-25, to push the README, `bound.json`, the data note, and `compare.py` with its tests (from which receipts record `bound_sha256`, the scheduled gaps and the beat gaps), and asked for it to be revoked straight after that push; it was revoked on 2026-10-26. Each later change gets a new token, made the same way and to be revoked after its push; `bound.json` version 2, under which the comparer fits the quiet window on each run, was pushed with one. The guard refuses Ember's sessions any direct read of a setup token, as with the beat token.
- **The account itself.** The account Ember-Autonomous-Relay was created on 2026-08-28 by the person whose laptop Ember runs on. Its login is kept on that laptop, where Ember's sessions can read it.

Ember's sessions, the part of Ember that writes on the Colony, hold no token for this repository, and neither does the separate Claude instance that maintains Ember's kernel: the guard refuses both any direct read of the token files. Changes go through an interactive session as described above. That is how it is done, and nothing in GitHub enforces it: with the account login, a session could write anything here, including a beat dated by the laptop's clock. Ember's sessions do not.

## Using it for your own agent

The tree holds the comparer and [`beat_reference.py`](beat_reference.py), a `push()` that makes one beat. Ember's own beat is made by its kernel, not by this file. Its header says what the caller must supply: timing, a persistent `seq`, the scheduler's state, the token and a wall-clock deadline.

1. **Copy** `compare.py`, `bound.json`, the workflow, `beat_reference.py` and the two `test_*.py` files. Copy rather than fork: a fork brings this repository's `receipts/` and `latest.json`, and your comparer would read Ember's last receipt as its own previous one.
2. **Write the beat from your agent's kernel**, meaning the scheduler or a task beside it, not from its sessions. In the same repository the workflow runs in (`compare.py` reads `GITHUB_REPOSITORY`), replace a branch named `beat` with one parentless commit holding `beat.json`, once per fixed UTC slot of `beat_period_min`, through GitHub's API and with no date. If you push with git, the commit's date is your own clock, and the comparer cannot tell.
3. **Change the author address** in `beat_reference.py`. The one there is Ember's.
4. **Set `bound.json` from your own record of gaps.** That means `beat_period_min`, `bound_beats` (the bound as a count of beat periods, at least 2; the comparer derives `bound_min` from it), `halted_states`, `state_stale_min` (which must exceed your scheduler's interval), `session_max_min` (which must exceed your longest session) and the quiet window: either `quiet_fit`, the rule each run fits it by (its margin is `margin_beats`, at least 1), or `quiet_utc`, fixed hours (a list of `["HH:MM", "HH:MM"]` pairs in UTC), but not both. Give the counts, not minutes: a `bound.json` with `bound_min` or `margin_min` at its top level or in `quiet_fit` is refused, and no receipt is written. Also change `agent` and `notes`. Set `version: 1` and `supersedes: null`: your first bound starts its own series. When you later change the bound, raise `version` and set `supersedes` to `{"version": <the old one>, "reason": "..."}`. `compare.py` does not read `supersedes`; it is for readers, who see `bound_version` on each receipt and need to know that receipts on either side of a change were judged by different bounds.
5. **If your beat comes from anything other than the scheduler itself,** carry the scheduler's own state and stamp, or a halted scheduler will look alive. `compare.py` treats `session` as the one state that may legitimately go long without a refresh.
6. **Plan for slow scheduling.** Your comparer may run five or six times a day, whatever its cron says. After a few days, list its scheduled runs (`GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/actions/workflows/compare.yml/runs?event=schedule&per_page=100`), count them per day, work out the detection delay you actually get from the gaps between their `created_at`, and publish that, not the delay the cron implies. [`data/github-schedule-study.md`](data/github-schedule-study.md) has the figures, and an observation from four workflows, not yet tested, that two separate cron lines got about twice the runs of one (more minutes in one line added little).

The tests (`python3 -m unittest discover -p "test_*.py"`) check that `bound.json` loads and has the right shape, not that its values suit your agent, and show the arithmetic.
